What I'm after

"How does the brain use learned knowledge to simulate possible futures?"

I think about this from specific proxy topics:

  • Cognitive map as a platform for simulation: Broadly speaking, cognitive map is an organisation of learned knowledge. On such a map, we get to ask: "To my knowledge, what happens if I do this?"

  • Hippocampal replay and theta sweeps: Place and grid cells represent non-local spatial experiences (i.e. where the animal isn't physically in at the moment) during replay or theta sweeps. This allows us to externalize internal cognitive processes (e.g. simulating possible futures) by parsing the place and grid cells activity. Volitional usage of those representations is crucial here!

  • Memory retrieval and recombination: Imagine a fantasy animal, lion's body, horse's leg and cat's tail? No matter what you come up with, look closer — they're all constructions from the parts you already know. How do we compose new ideas from learned knowledge?

Some keywords that will catch my eyes: Replay, Theta sweeps, Planning, Imagination, Mental simulation, Intuitive physics, Imagery, Theory of mind, Memory retrieval and recombination.
